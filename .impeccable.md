# Impeccable — design context for Tokenomics

## Design Context

### Users

Two audiences, weighted toward the first.

1. **Buildathon judges**, seeing this on a projector for roughly fifteen seconds before
   they read a word. They are not the buyer; they are deciding whether the idea is real.
   The Statement view must land one idea hard and be legible from across a room.
2. **Developers who would actually use it** — people paying for Claude Code who suspect
   they are wasting money and want to know where. They come back repeatedly, want density,
   and will click into evidence because they distrust round numbers.

The job to be done: *"show me where my AI spend actually went, and tell me one thing to
change."* Not "give me a dashboard."

The emotional arc is specific and it is the whole product: **recognition → wince →
relief.** The user sees their own real numbers (recognition), realises how little of the
spend was productive work (wince), and is handed a concrete fix with a dollar figure
(relief). Any design decision that blunts that arc is wrong, however pretty.

### Brand Personality

**Candid. Precise. Dry.**

The voice is an advisor, not an accountant and never a scold. It says "you ran the same
curl 11 times" rather than "loop detected". It is willing to deliver bad news plainly and
equally willing to say "this habit is working, keep it" — an advisor that only ever
scolds is not credible, and one of the strongest cards in the product is a compliment.

Numbers are the argument. Every claim carries evidence a skeptic can open. Confidence is
labelled honestly (high / medium / heuristic) rather than implied uniformly. Estimated
figures must look estimated.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Dark, but committed and warm — not the default dark.** The user asked to keep the
existing vibe while making it simpler and more readable. So: dark-first, deliberately
re-tinted away from the sky-on-near-black palette it started with, which is exactly the
generic-AI look listed as an anti-reference.

- Neutrals are **warm charcoal**, tinted toward the accent hue — never pure black, never
  a blue-grey. Pure `#000` and `#fff` are banned.
- **One** accent, used sparingly and meaningfully: ochre/amber for attention. A second
  reserved hue for burn (loss) and one for cash (saved). Colour carries meaning; it is
  never decoration.
- **Readability over density on the Statement view; density is fine everywhere else.**
  Simpler compositions, fewer competing elements, larger type, more air.
- Type does the work: a strong hierarchy with real size contrast, tabular figures
  everywhere numbers appear, tight tracking on display sizes.
- Restraint in motion. One well-orchestrated entrance beats scattered micro-interactions.

**Anti-references — this must not look like:**
- **A generic AI dashboard.** No cyan-on-dark, no purple-to-blue gradients, no
  glassmorphism, no identical rounded cards repeating down a grid, no glow borders.
- **A crypto or trading terminal.** "Tokenomics" must not read as crypto. No neon
  green/red, no candlesticks, no hype.
- **An enterprise BI tool.** No grey chrome, no toolbar of dropdowns, no charts without
  a point of view.

**Theme:** a single committed dark theme. No toggle. Executed precisely rather than
twice, half-well.

### Design Principles

1. **The number is the hero, the sentence is the argument.** Every screen states one
   thing in words a person would say out loud, with the figure that proves it. If a view
   cannot be summarised in one sentence, it is doing too much.
2. **Never show a dollar figure without the evidence behind it.** Confidence is labelled,
   estimates look like estimates, and the evidence drawer is real because someone will
   open it.
3. **Colour means something.** Ochre draws the eye to what matters, burn marks loss, cash
   marks what is working. A colour used decoratively is a bug.
4. **Simplify toward legibility.** When a composition and its readability conflict, the
   projector wins. Fewer elements, bigger type, more air — especially on Statement.
5. **Earn the wince honestly.** No inflated savings, no invented findings, no metric
   chosen because it sounds worse than it is. The product's entire credibility rests on
   the numbers being real and reproducible from the user's own machine.
